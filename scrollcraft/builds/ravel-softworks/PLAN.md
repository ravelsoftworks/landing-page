# Plan

## Grammar: Chaptered editorial (§2.2)

The page is a printed engineering proposal. Chapters with roman numerals are the
unit. Grounds hard-cut between paper and navy; they are painted per section, so
there is no drift. A folio sits in the margin (desktop: left edge; phone: a thin
line at the bottom) and shows the current chapter; it opens a contents list.
There is no fixed marketing bar.

Why the other seven lost:
- Filmic one-shot: the user asked for distinct chapters, and a buyer needs to scan.
- Live surface: we have no product. A services firm faking a dashboard breaks the honesty rule.
- Continuous world: the user chose chapters over one world.
- Typographic poster: there is too much substance (services, method, FAQ) for type alone.
- Gallery/catalog: the services are an argument, not a range of objects.
- Split stage: there is no single two-sided comparison to carry the whole page.
- Rhythmic cutlist: it is an energy grammar. The brief says "professional and elegant".

Deviation, stated: the grammar says "no media above the fold". The title page
carries a drawn frontispiece plate (the isometric R), because the standing
hero-depth preference needs independent planes. The plate is a technical
drawing on the paper ground, in its own column with a caption. Media never
bleeds under the type.

## Hero layer contract (title page)

| Plane | Asset | Movement | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Far | isometric dot grid (SVG) | slowest, plus pointer 4px | never under the headline at full strength |
| Mid | the R, as 33 voxels in 3 disciplines | exploded view settles into solid as you scroll (pin 1.7) | shared ground shadow stays on the plinth |
| Callouts | leader lines + labels: Software, AI & automation, IT & cloud | draw in as the R settles | real HTML labels |
| Near | 3 loose blocks, larger, slightly blurred | fastest, cross the plate edges | never over the H1 |
| Type | H1, one line of sub copy, Start a project, contents | stable | readable at p = 0 |

Opening: the exploded R, parts hovering apart, the headline beside it.
Midpoint: the parts close in; the callouts draw.
Exit: the solid R on its plinth, with three labelled disciplines. It cuts to navy.

## Signature move

**The block builder.** The visitor toggles what they need (seven modules), where
they are starting from, and how soon. Each module is a coloured course of blocks
that drops onto a plinth in the same isometric drawing language as the hero, so
their project literally gets built. A running brief (plain sentences, computed
from the selection) writes itself beside it. "Start a project with this brief"
carries the selection into the Netlify form: the checkboxes stay ticked and the
message is pre-written, and it is still fully editable.
