# Ravel Softworks brand guide

Short and practical: how to use the logo, colours, type and the block system
so everything we make looks like it came from the same company.

## The idea

**We build the right parts and fit them together.** The mark is an R made of
isometric blocks, and the blocks are the brand. A block always *means*
something: a service, a step, a part of a client's system. Never decoration.

## Logo

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `ravel-mark.svg` | The mark on its own. Vector, use it everywhere possible (web, slides, favicon) |
| `ravel-mark-256.png` | Only where SVG is not accepted |
| `ravel-logo-full.png` | Mark with the RAVEL SOFTWORKS wordmark (square, for profile pictures) |

- Next to text, the wordmark is "Ravel **Softworks**" set in Geist (Softworks in semibold).
- Clear space around the mark: at least half its width on every side.
- Minimum size: 16 px tall on screen, 8 mm in print.
- Works on paper (#F3F1EB), white and navy (#0C1B36). Do not place it on photos
  or on the teal, blue or indigo brand colours.
- Never recolour, outline, rotate, stretch, add shadows to, or redraw the mark.

## Colour

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#F3F1EB` | Main background |
| Navy | `#0C1B36` | Text on paper, dark sections, primary buttons |
| Ink soft | `#4A5670` | Secondary text on paper |
| Teal (deep) | `#0A6E65` | Links and accents on paper (passes contrast) |
| Teal (bright) | `#3DD6C3` | Accents on navy |
| Block teal | `#12B8A6` · top `#5FE6D2` · side `#0B7F80` | Blocks only |
| Block blue | `#1E7BF2` · top `#6CC3FF` · side `#1A4FC4` | Blocks only |
| Block indigo | `#4A52E6` · top `#8C92FF` · side `#3036B0` | Blocks only |

- One accent per surface: deep teal on paper, bright teal on navy.
- The three block colours are for blocks, never for text or backgrounds.
- No gradients on text, no glows, no purple-to-blue "AI" gradients.

## Type

- **Geist** for everything (headlines 600, body 400). **Geist Mono** only for
  small labels and data (uppercase, letter-spaced).
- Headlines are tightly tracked and short. Sentence case, never all caps.
- No em dashes; use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets.

## The block system

1. **One angle.** Always the same isometric view as the logo (30°), light from
   the top: the top face is lightest, the left face is the base colour, and the
   right face is darker.
2. **Meaning first.** Every block stands for something:
   - a **stack** = teams or layers working as one (software, AI, IT)
   - **dashed outline** = a part that is missing or still to come
   - **duplicate** = repeated, manual work
   - **lone block** = a tool that isn't connected to anything
   - **blocks on a plinth** = one platform, several places or parts
   - **a stack that grows** = progress through a project
3. **One block moment per screen, page or slide.** If everything has blocks,
   nothing does.
4. **Nothing floats without a reason.** Floating blocks are allowed only when
   they are about to become something (the hero, a build-up animation).
5. **Edges step, they don't slant.** Where two areas meet, a stepped edge (like
   stacked blocks) is on-brand; diagonal or wavy dividers are not.
6. **Buttons have a block edge**: a thin darker side at the bottom right that
   the button presses into.

## Voice

Plain, direct, specific. Say what we will do and what the client gets. No
invented numbers, no testimonials we don't have, no "revolutionise",
"seamless" or "next-gen".

## Where to use it next

- LinkedIn banner and post templates on paper or navy, with one block composition each.
- Proposal, invoice and slide templates: navy title slide with the mark,
  paper content slides, block icons for services.
- Business cards: the mark on navy on the front, and details on paper on the back.
